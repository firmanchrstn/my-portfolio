01. Background

Context & Background

Mobile JKN serves as the digital backbone of Indonesia’s National Health Insurance (BPJS Kesehatan), catering to over 200 million users. While functionally comprehensive, the application faces significant challenges in managing its vast administrative data.

As an Information Systems student, I initiated this redesign project to address the growing "Complexity Gap"—where bureaucratic density often hinders the speed and ease of healthcare access. The goal was to transform a complex administrative tool into a seamless, intuitive digital health assistant.

02. Challenge

Problem Statement

Public service applications often suffer from “Information Overload,” where excessive and poorly structured information reduces usability and increases user frustration. Through my analysis, I identified three major pain points: first, cognitive overload, caused by dense administrative data regarding registration, rights, and obligations that made it difficult for users to quickly access relevant information during urgent situations; second, perceived performance friction, where cluttered and unorganized layouts created the impression of a heavy and slow application, ultimately increasing user anxiety; and third, weak information hierarchy, in which critical data such as digital membership status and ID numbers were buried beneath secondary information, reducing clarity and accessibility for users.

03. Innovation

The Solution

My approach focused on simplifying the user experience through a more structured and efficient system. I redesigned the interface using a card-based information architecture to improve readability, applied progressive disclosure with tabs and accordions to reduce cognitive load, enhanced the Digital Participant Card with clearer visual hierarchy for faster verification, and optimized the FAQ and service channels to encourage user self-service and autonomy.
