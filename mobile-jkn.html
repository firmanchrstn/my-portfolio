crisis_alert The Challenge

The "Dawn Queue" Phenomenon & Uncertainty

Healthcare facilities in Indonesia face a systemic challenge: physical overcrowding in outpatient waiting rooms. Although the Mobile JKN app already exists, the current system remains passive—it only displays a queue number without a dynamic estimate of when the patient will be called.

As a result, patients battle uncertainty by arriving much earlier (the dawn queue). This condition not only diminishes recovery quality and triggers emotional fatigue, but also raises the risk of cross-infection (healthcare-associated infections) in crowded waiting rooms.

lightbulb The Innovation

From Passive to Proactive

What if a health app did not need to be constantly stared at by a patient who is unwell? This project proposes a redesign of Mobile JKN's queue monitoring interface by combining design empathy (User-Centered Design) with analytical intelligence (Machine Learning).

The goal is to shift the user's cognitive load onto the backend system, creating a queue monitoring experience that is more humane and inclusive.

Key Features

Three Layers of a New Experience

space_dashboard

User-Centered Queue Dashboard

Information navigation was completely restructured to be more concise and clear. The new visual structure reduces confusion for everyday users with varying levels of digital literacy.

query_stats

AI-Powered Wait Time Prediction

Integration of a simulated Machine Learning (regression) model on the backend to predict service duration in real-time based on the hospital's historical data.

notifications_active

Proactive Pop-up Notifications

ML prediction results are used to send smart pop-up notifications in stages—starting with a "Get Ready" recommendation and progressing to "Leave Now" for the healthcare facility.

Impact & Value

Toward a Calmer Healthcare Ecosystem

This prototype offers a new vision for the BPJS Kesehatan ecosystem. For patients, it means freedom from the stress of waiting without certainty and the ability to rest longer at home. For hospitals (case study: Metro Hospital Cikarang), this innovation has strong potential to reduce waiting room congestion, improve operational efficiency, and restore healthcare facilities to their true function as places of recovery.
